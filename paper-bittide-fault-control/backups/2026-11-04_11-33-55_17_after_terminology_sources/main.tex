\begin{abstract}
Finite receiving buffers couple bittide synchronization to transient
resource use. We propose a decentralized incremental fuzzy
proportional--integral (PI) controller
that computes frequency corrections from occupancy error and its sample
change. We specify the complete inference law, derive an implementation-
consistent PI calibration and characterize the central nonlinear map.
For a synchronous zero-latency fluid reference, invariant-domain and
Lyapunov analysis, together with interval enclosures and a contracting
tail, certifies an all-time excursion below 17.315 frames for one
prescribed initialization, versus approximately 23.404 for matched-gain
PI. Continuous-phase Callisto separately evaluates delayed local
execution. Matched-gain startup tests show smaller buffer excursions;
after equal-budget selection from 24 candidates per family, 186 held-out
tests show mean paired disagreement-RMS improvements of 16.78\% and
7.85\% in two bounded-random latency regimes. The improvement is
regime-dependent and carries a common-frequency-shift cost. These
results connect a specified nonlinear mechanism to conditional
buffer-headroom and synchronization benefits, while distinguishing
reference guarantees from finite-time delayed execution.
\end{abstract}
