\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Distributed cyber-physical computation requires nodes running on independent clocks to coordinate their computations and communications. For applications that depend on reproducible execution, the relationships between communicated events must remain well defined as physical timing varies. This creates a fundamental implementation challenge: a coherent logical execution must be realized using oscillators, communication links and storage resources whose physical behavior is neither identical nor unlimited.

Logical synchrony provides a framework for addressing this challenge without distributing a global physical clock. In bittide, continuous frame exchange and elastic receiving buffers connect the physical operation of neighboring nodes to their logical execution. Each node adjusts its oscillator frequency using locally observed buffer occupancies. These buffers consequently serve two roles: they provide the feedback signal for synchronization and absorb the imbalance between incoming frames and local consumption. The storage available to accommodate that imbalance is finite~\cite{lall2024logicalSynchrony}.

The resulting control problem concerns the entire trajectory towards an operating point. Initial oscillator differences and changes in frame-delivery time can consume buffer headroom before the network settles or while it responds to a disturbance. A small eventual frequency disagreement does not by itself establish that the intervening buffer trajectory remains admissible. The objective is therefore to use local occupancy feedback to reduce frequency disagreement and control deviations from compatible occupancy targets, while respecting finite buffer capacity and oscillator-correction limits. This formulation makes transient buffer excursion, convergence and control activity joint criteria for evaluating a controller.

Existing bittide research establishes important foundations for this problem. PI performance analysis~\cite{lall2022resistance} relates closed-loop behavior to controller gains and network resistance distances. Fluid occupancy analysis~\cite{lall2024bufferOccupancy} characterizes steady buffer levels, and reframing methods~\cite{lall2023bufferCentering} address buffer centering. Our earlier study, \emph{Stabilizing the bittide by Tuning Controllers Under Topology and Latency Constraints}, examined PI gain selection under topology and fixed-delay constraints. Building on these results, the question addressed here is how a local nonlinear Fuzzy PI law can reshape transient resource use under comparable scales, memory and update conventions. In particular, the connection between the nonlinear rule surface, a provable buffer-excursion benefit and behavior under delivery perturbations requires explicit analysis and controlled comparison.

We propose a decentralized occupancy-based Fuzzy PI controller for bittide. Each node computes an frequency correction increment from its receiving-buffer error and the update-to-update change in that error. In the central input region studied here, the proposed surface strengthens its calibrated linear counterpart for same-sign inputs and attenuates its magnitude for opposing-sign inputs. This direction-dependent action provides flexibility at fixed calibrated PI gain coordinates. It motivates investigating whether the nonlinear correction can reduce transient buffer demands while retaining convergence, and determining how its effects on frequency disagreement and control activity depend on the operating conditions.

This paper presents the complete controller, establishes its con\-tinuous-clock realization and combines nonlinear analysis with evaluation to investigate that question. We derive the exact central-region map and its scale correspondence with the PI baseline, and verify implementation equivalence when the nonlinear map is replaced by its calibrated linear counterpart. For a synchronous, zero-latency fluid reference, we establish an invariant input region and a common Lyapunov certificate. An outward interval enclosure of the nonlinear trajectory, followed by a contracting infinite-tail bound, provides an all-time buffer-excursion guarantee. For one prescribed three-node initialization at matched gain coordinates, the Fuzzy excursion is certified below 17.315 fluid frames, whereas PI attains an exact peak of approximately 23.403701 frames. This establishes a strict buffer-headroom benefit for that reference and initialization.

We separately evaluate the proposed law in continuous-phase Callisto,
including integer buffer events, local-clock polling and delayed
correction application. Matched-gain startup tests show smaller buffer
excursions for Fuzzy at three prescribed initial frequency mismatches.
We then give PI and fixed-shape Fuzzy the same 24-candidate,
four-case tuning budget, freeze the selected controllers, and execute
186 held-out tests using shared delivery profiles. In the two
original bounded-random regimes, the random-priority Fuzzy controller
reduces mean paired disagreement-RMS ratios by 16.78\% and 7.85\%
relative to the independently selected PI, with lower mean command
activity. The burst-priority Fuzzy controller also reduces IAE by
approximately 55.8\% in the larger held-out burst. These gains have
a defined operating scope: common-frequency shift is a cost, and a
smaller burst and a separately registered slower-random regime do not
share the improvement. The combined evidence establishes conditional
transient and synchronization benefits while keeping the reference
guarantee distinct from measured delayed execution.

The contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A decentralized incremental Fuzzy PI controller for bittide,
  with complete inference rules, continuous-clock realization,
  implementation-checked PI calibration and an exact central-region
  nonlinear characterization.
  \item An invariant-domain and Lyapunov analysis for the stated fluid
  reference, with bounds on physical frequencies and applied corrections,
  and a strict all-time reduction in buffer excursion for one
  prescribed initialization at matched gains.
  \item Continuous-phase Callisto evaluation combining matched-gain
  startup stress, equal-budget independent tuning and held-out
  paired tests, identifying operating conditions with lower
  disagreement RMS or transient buffer demand and their associated
  performance tradeoffs.
\end{enumerate}
